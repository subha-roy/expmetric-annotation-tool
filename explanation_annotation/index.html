Quick reference

Task A — one explanation

  • Correctness → Is the explanation right?
  • Grounding → Are its statements visibly supported?
  • Clarity → Is it easy to understand?
  • Usefulness → Does it explain WHY the pair aligns/fails?
  • Unsupported Content → Did it assert something unsupported?

Task B — a highlighted region

  • Localization → Are the highlighted regions correct?
  • Sufficiency → Do they contain enough evidence to judge the claim?

Every 1–5 question shows all five text labels, not just the ends — 3 always means partial / mixed, while 1 and 5 are the clear extremes. Read the label you're clicking, not just the number.

A · What you are doing

For every sample you see an image and the caption being evaluated. Four different automatic systems each wrote a short explanation of whether the image supports the caption. The four explanations are shown to you only as Explanation A / B / C / D — you are never told which real system wrote which, and the order is different for every sample.

  1. Task A — rate each of the 4 explanations, one at a time. No highlighted regions are shown yet.
  2. Task B — once all 4 explanations are rated, a second panel opens (only for the explanation(s) whose system actually produced a highlighted image region for a claim — this may be none, one, or more than one explanation depending on the sample) where you rate up to 3 highlighted regions. Once Task B is revealed, your Task A answers for that sample lock and can no longer be changed — this keeps the two judgements independent.

Not every explanation has a Task B — some systems here do not produce image regions at all. That is expected and not something to flag.

B · Task A — rating one explanation (full wording)

Diagnostic Correctness“How accurately does the explanation identify the relevant matches or mismatches between the image and caption?”
1 Completely incorrect · 2 Mostly incorrect · 3 Partially correct · 4 Mostly correct · 5 Completely correct
Visual Grounding“How well are the statements in the explanation supported by what is visibly present in the image and stated in the caption?”
1 Not supported · 2 Mostly unsupported · 3 Partially supported · 4 Mostly supported · 5 Fully supported
Clarity“How easy is the explanation to understand, without unnecessary or confusing information?”
1 Very unclear · 2 Somewhat unclear · 3 Neither clear nor unclear · 4 Clear · 5 Very clear
Diagnostic Usefulness(primary question) “How useful is the explanation for understanding why the image and caption align or fail to align?”
1 Not useful · 2 Slightly useful · 3 Moderately useful · 4 Very useful · 5 Extremely useful
Unsupported Content“Does this explanation contain any specific factual statement that is contradicted by, or unsupported by, the image/caption?” — Yes / No / Unsure

Judge every explanation against the image yourself — do not assume a confident-sounding explanation is a correct one.

C · Task B — rating a highlighted region (full wording)

Up to 3 claims per qualifying explanation, each with its own highlighted box on the image and its own short claim text.

Evidence Localization Accuracy“How accurately do the highlighted region(s) identify the visual evidence relevant to this claim?”
1 Completely incorrect · 2 Mostly incorrect · 3 Partially correct · 4 Mostly correct · 5 Completely correct
Evidence Sufficiency“How sufficient are the highlighted region(s) for deciding whether this claim is supported by the image?”
1 Completely insufficient · 2 Mostly insufficient · 3 Partially sufficient · 4 Mostly sufficient · 5 Fully sufficient

D · Skip for now / Technical issue

Skip for nowPostpone the whole sample. It stays in your assignment and turns red on the dashboard until you finish it.
Technical issueSomething is actually broken — image failed to load, garbled text, or the app misbehaving.

E · Navigating, saving and exporting

  • Your work saves automatically — you can close the tab and return.
  • Completed samples can be reopened, but once Task B has revealed for a sample its Task A answers stay locked (Task B ratings can still be revised).
  • Use the same browser and device where practical, and do not clear site data before exporting.
  • Download backup from the menu at any time; when everything is finished, use Export final annotations and send us that file.
