Annotation guide

Worked examples

Three complete image–text pairs, annotated with exactly this rubric by GPT-5.6-Sol: every atomic statement carries both judgements and a short reason, and each pair ends with an overall score. They are the fastest way to calibrate before you start.

Practice only — they are not benchmark samples, never count towards your progress, and never appear in your export.

A · What you are doing

For every sample you see an image, the original text, and the atomic statements extracted from that text. Your task is to:

  1. for each atomic statement give two independent judgements — whether it is a good decomposition of the text, and how strongly the image supports it;
  2. then, once every statement has both, give one overall text–image alignment score for the pair.

There is no right answer key. We are measuring careful human judgement, so be consistent with yourself rather than trying to guess what somebody else would say.

B · How to read an atomic statement

Statements are written as short phrases, not full sentences — you will see green hay, not “The hay is green.” Each phrase carries exactly one claim; read it as an assertion about the scene the text describes.

objectsomething exists — a wooden bench
attributea property bound to something — red shirt
counthow many — three dogs
actionwhat is happening — the woman is pouring tea
relationwho does what to whom — the dog chasing the cat
spatialwhere things are — the lamp above the table

Direction and binding matter. the dog chasing the cat is a different claim from the cat chasing the dog, and red shirt, blue hat is different from blue shirt, red hat. Judge the statement exactly as written. If a statement is negative — no people — it is supported when the image really does show none.

C · Judgement 1 — Decomposition quality (text only)

Is this statement a good atomic unit of the original text? This question has nothing to do with the image — answer it as if you had never seen the picture.

ReasonableA single, self-contained claim that the text really makes.
Needs splitCarries more than one claim that should be judged separately.
Needs mergeToo fragmentary to mean anything on its own; belongs with another part.
RedundantRepeats a claim another part already covers.
Not entailedStates something the text does not actually say.

A statement can be a perfectly good decomposition and still be contradicted by the image, or a poor decomposition that the image happens to support. Keep the two judgements apart.

D · Judgement 2 — Image support (image only)

How strongly does the image support that same statement, taken exactly as written?

0Not supported / contradicted. The image clearly does not support the statement, or clearly shows something incompatible with it.
1Weakly supported. Little supporting evidence; the image is mostly inconsistent with the statement.
2Partially supported. Part of the claim holds, but an important part is missing, unclear or inconsistent.
3Strongly supported. Mostly supported, with only minor uncertainty or incompleteness.
4Fully supported. Clearly and completely supported.
?Cannot judge. The image genuinely does not give enough evidence — severe occlusion, the content is outside the frame, or the view is simply insufficient.

Score the complete atomic statement, including attributes, counts, bindings, roles, and relations. A statement is only fully supported when every element of it holds — the right object, the right attribute bound to it, the right number, and the right direction.

Give the image-support score even when you marked the statement Needs split, Needs merge, Redundant or Not entailed. The two judgements are separate, and statements are never rewritten.

Worked examples

red shirt image clearly shows a red shirt → 4 · the shirt is red but partly in shadow → 3 · image clearly shows a blue shirt → 0 · the shirt is mostly hidden and its colour cannot reasonably be determined → Cannot judge

three dogs exactly three clearly visible dogs → 4 · three dogs but one is heavily cropped → 3 · only two dogs visible → 1 · no dogs at all → 0

the dog chasing the cat both animals present and the direction is clear → 4 · both present but the direction is genuinely ambiguous → 2 · the cat is plainly chasing the dog → 0

These are calibration aids, not rules for every ambiguous case — use the scale consistently and trust your judgement.

E · Overall alignment (1–5)

After the statements, judge the whole pair: how well does the image represent the text as a whole?

1Not aligned. The image substantially contradicts the text or fails to represent its important content.
2Weakly aligned. Some relevant content is present, but major parts of the text are incorrect, missing or inconsistent.
3Partially aligned. The image and text match in some important respects, but there are clear mismatches, missing information or unsupported content.
4Well aligned. Most of the text is correctly represented, with only minor mismatches, omissions or uncertainty.
5Fully aligned. The image clearly and accurately represents the complete text, with no meaningful mismatch.

Extra objects or details that the text does not mention should not automatically reduce the score. A caption describes a scene; it is not required to be an exhaustive inventory of it. Lower the score for what the text claims and the image does not deliver — contradictions, missing important content, wrong attributes, counts, relations or directions — not for additional things that simply happen to be in the picture. Extra content matters only when it actively conflicts with the text.

A clearly missing or incorrect major object, count, action, participant, or relation is a meaningful mismatch and should not normally be treated as a minor omission when choosing an overall score of 4.

Do not mechanically average the atomic scores. A single wrong binding in a short text can matter more than several minor omissions in a long one. This is your judgement of the complete pair after inspecting the details.

F · Cannot judge vs Skip for now vs Technical issue

Cannot judgeI can see the sample normally, but the image genuinely does not give enough evidence for this one statement.
Skip for nowI want to postpone the whole sample and come back to it later. It stays in your assignment, shows red on the dashboard, and the app moves you on to the next sample.
Technical issueSomething is actually broken — the image will not load, the image is corrupt, the text is malformed, or the app misbehaves.

These are three different things. Cannot judge is not a way to avoid a hard decision, and a broken sample is not a skip.
