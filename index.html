Annotation guide

A · What you are doing

For every sample you see an image, the original text, and the atomic statements extracted from that text. Your task is to:

  1. for each atomic statement give two judgements — whether it is a good decomposition of the text, and how strongly the image supports it;
  2. then, once every statement has both, give one overall text–image alignment score.

B · Decomposition quality — judged against the text

First, is this statement a good atomic unit of the original text? This has nothing to do with the image.

ReasonableA good atomic visual unit.
Needs splitContains multiple claims that should be separated.
Needs mergeShould be combined with another part to make a meaningful unit.
RedundantDuplicates information already represented by another part.
Not entailedIntroduces information the text does not actually state.

C · Image support — judged against the image

Second, and independently: how strongly does the image support that same statement?

0Unsupported / contradicted
1Weak support
2Partial support
3Strong support
4Full support
?Cannot judge

Give the image-support score even when you marked the statement Needs split, Needs merge, Redundant or Not entailed. The two judgements are separate, and the statements are never rewritten.

Full definitions
  • 0 — Not supported / contradicted. The image clearly does not support the statement, or clearly shows something incompatible with it.
  • 1 — Weakly supported. Little supporting visual evidence; the image is mostly inconsistent with the statement.
  • 2 — Partially supported. Some of the statement is supported, but an important part is missing, unclear or inconsistent.
  • 3 — Strongly supported. Mostly supported, with only minor uncertainty or incompleteness.
  • 4 — Fully supported. Clearly and completely supported.
  • Cannot judge. The image genuinely does not give enough visual evidence — severe occlusion, the relevant content is outside the frame, or the view is simply insufficient.

Examples

red shirt image clearly shows a red shirt → 4 · image clearly shows a blue shirt → 0 · the shirt is mostly hidden and its colour cannot reasonably be determined → Cannot judge

three dogs exactly three clearly visible dogs → 4 · only two dogs visible → a low score on the scale above, chosen consistently for the evidence you can actually see

These are guides, not rules for every ambiguous case — use the scale consistently and trust your judgement.

D · Overall alignment (1–5)

1Not aligned. The image substantially contradicts the text or fails to represent its important content.
2Weakly aligned. Some relevant content is present, but major parts of the text are incorrect, missing or inconsistent.
3Partially aligned. The image and text match in some important respects, but there are clear mismatches, missing information or unsupported content.
4Well aligned. Most of the text is correctly represented, with only minor mismatches, omissions or uncertainty.
5Fully aligned. The image clearly and accurately represents the complete text, with no meaningful mismatch.

Do not mechanically average the atomic scores. This is your judgement of the complete image–text pair after inspecting the details.

E · Cannot judge vs Skip for now vs Technical issue

Cannot judgeI can see the sample normally, but the image genuinely does not give enough evidence for this one statement.
Skip for nowI want to postpone the whole sample and come back to it later. It stays in your assignment and shows red on the dashboard.
Technical issueSomething is actually broken — the image will not load, the image is corrupt, the text is malformed, or the app misbehaves.

These are three different things. Cannot judge is not a way to avoid a hard decision, and a broken sample is not a skip.
